01 · The anatomy of the polycrisis

Crises that multiply, not add up

A polycrisis is what happens when separate global risks become entangled, so that each one amplifies the others. A heatwave overloads a grid. The blackout spoils vaccines and shuts water pumps. Contaminated water spreads disease among children already weakened by hunger. The combined human cost is far greater than the sum of each crisis viewed alone.

03 · Seven survival pathways

Where the 368 million lives come from

Coordinated resilience works through seven survival pathways. Clean air and healthcare are the two largest lifesavers, together accounting for 63% of deaths prevented. Water and nutrition save the youngest lives, preserving the most healthy years per death averted.

×

The Resilience Multiplier

Some investments unlock others. Electrifying a rural clinic powers vaccine fridges, medical oxygen, lighting for night-time births, water pumps, phone-based early warnings, and cold chains for food, a six-way multiplier. That is why coordinated action saves 136 million more lives than the same ministries acting in silos.

No life counted twice

Many people face several risks at once: a child exposed to smoky stoves and dirty water and malnutrition can only be saved once. The model removes this overlap with a formal 21.4% discount.

04 · Beyond mortality

The Human Suffering Reduction Dividend

Counting deaths misses most of the story. The HSRD measures improvements in daily living: years lived with electricity, clean cooking, safe water, sanitation, enough food and primary healthcare, and years lived without extreme climate vulnerability.

What's a person-year?

One person living in better conditions for one full year. Helping 10 million people for 20 years = 200 million person-years.

The report keeps four ledgers (deaths, healthy life-years, and two HSRD ledgers) and never adds them together.

05 · A widening survival wedge

How the benefits build, decade by decade

2025–2034

The First Decade

6M net lives

Infrastructure takes time, so early wins come from fast tools: phone-based early warnings, oral rehydration salts, vaccination catch-up, maternal micronutrients and point-of-use water chlorination.

2035–2054

The Scale-Up Decades

74M net lives

Universal electrification, clean cooking for 2 billion people, piped urban water and primary clinic networks reach critical mass. Annual lives saved pass 4.8 million by 2050.

2055–2100

The Mature Dividend

289M net lives

Built systems operate at scale and warming is held near 1.85°C. Lives saved peak at 6.6 million a year around 2071, about 18,000 every day.

06 · The geographical priority

89% of lives saved are in the Global South

Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia together account for 62% of lives saved. Africa's population is projected to grow from 1.3 to 3.5 billion by 2100, so foundations built today protect far more people tomorrow.

Spotlight

Sub-Saharan Africa

91 lives saved per 1,000 residents

Nearly four times the rate for regions outside Africa and South Asia. More than four in five people without electricity live here, so solar mini-grids and clean cooking alone prevent about 34 million net deaths.

Lead pathways: healthcare, food, water, clean cooking.

08 · The high cost of waiting

Every year of delay forfeits 8.3 million lives

Waiting until 2040 cuts lives saved from 368 million to 225 million, and from 58 million to just 7 million by 2050. That's about 16 preventable deaths every minute of inaction.

09 · A practical playbook

Ten moves for leaders

Concrete, time-bound actions drawn from the report's leader playbook.

  1. Universal multi-hazard early warnings by 2032

    Close the cell-broadcast, radar and community evacuation gap. Cuts disaster mortality sixfold for under US$2B a year.

  2. A global clean-cooking sprint for 2.05B people

    Induction stoves and clean LPG with targeted micro-subsidies in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, 2026–2040. ~54M net lives.

  3. Electrify every clinic & unserved community by 2035

    Solar-plus-battery micro-grids for 100,000+ rural clinics first, while connecting the 666M people without power.

  4. Retire unabated coal & scrub urban air

    Renewable grids, smokestack scrubbers and electric transit, 2026–2045. ~78M additional ambient-air lives.

  5. Piped chlorinated water & sealed sanitation

    Prioritise dense urban slums and rural health districts to slash child diarrhoeal mortality by 84% before 2050.

  6. Primary healthcare & universal vaccination

    Community health workers, obstetric care, medical oxygen, malaria and childhood vaccines. 98M net lives.

  7. Digital shock-responsive safety nets by 2040

    Pre-register vulnerable households on mobile payment rails so cash flows automatically when droughts, floods or price spikes hit.

  8. Pathogen surveillance & 100-day vaccine hubs by 2035

    Genomic wastewater monitoring and distributed manufacturing. Halves the risk of a pandemic killing more than 50M (35% → 17%).

  9. Cool cities & protect outdoor workers

    Cool roofs, urban tree shade, work–rest heat standards and solar cooling centres across tropical megacities, 2028–2050.

  10. Climate-smart seeds, cold chains & first-1,000-days nutrition

    Maternal and infant nutrition plus drought-tolerant crops and solar cold storage. 26M net lives at ~US$1,597 per healthy year.

6%

Protect delivery capacity

Ring-fencing about 6% of resilience capital budgets for transparent procurement, staff retention and operations & maintenance keeps assets working and countries on the upper half of the survival trajectory.

10 · How the numbers were built

Method & caveats

Data sources

The model starts from UN and Global Burden of Disease projections: IHME GBD 2021/2023 and WHO Global Health Estimates (204 countries), UN World Population Prospects 2024, IPCC AR6, the Lancet Countdown 2025, and IEA, World Bank, FAO SOFI and WHO/UNICEF JMP infrastructure data.

Uncertainty

5,000 Monte Carlo simulations vary 28 uncertain inputs; 90% intervals are the 5th to 95th percentiles. Nine adverse stress tests probe delivery failures and worsening hazards. Present values use a 3% discount rate.

Evidence grades

A: hard data · B: strong peer-reviewed model · C: calibrated synthesis where records are incomplete · D: late-century stress-boundary scenarios (2060–2100).

Important caveat

All figures are scenario outputs under stated assumptions, not forecasts. They show what difference action could make, and what delay and fragmentation would forfeit.
