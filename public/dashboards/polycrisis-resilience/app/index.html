Polycrisis Resilience — Monitor of Compound Shock

The polycrisis is a coupling problem: heat, water, food, toxics and conflict transmit through the same fiscal and institutional channels, so each shock arrives against a buffer already spent by the last.

The binding constraint is not the magnitude of any individual stressor but the adaptive capacity available when they coincide. Regions with high exposure and thin capacity absorb the first shock and lose the capacity to absorb the second; regions with deep buffers convert the same shock into a manageable cost. This monitor tracks that divergence — shock exposure, adaptive capacity, institutional resilience, compound hazard load and resilience outlook — across three scenarios, because the difference between a managed century and a cascading one is decided in the capacity built before the shocks overlap.

Attribution & Method

Indices are qualitative 0–1 / −1..+1 constructions synthesized from cited datasets, not peer-reviewed projections.

Exposure baselines draw on WRI Aqueduct water stress, IPCC AR6 hazard exposure, UNEP adaptation finance gap reporting, and UN WPP population projections.
