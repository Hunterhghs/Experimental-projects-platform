Methods & sources

Sustainable Convergence is one of the six H Heuristics concepts: closing the income and living-standards gap between developing and high-income economies without repeating the environmental damage of past growth. Within the H Heuristics signature framework (working name; provisional), it is the destination the Development Multiplier aims at. This monitor is a proposed measurement approach, open to testing.

Evidence labels

Observed World Bank WDI data. Composite H Heuristics illustrative index built from observed data. Scenario mechanical extrapolations, not forecasts.

Sustainable Convergence Index (SCI) Composite

  • Development level (45%): mean of GDP per capita (log scale, $1k–$75k), life expectancy (45–85), under-5 mortality (log, 200–2 per 1,000), and access to electricity, clean cooking, basic water, basic sanitation and the internet (0–100%). At least five of eight required.
  • Convergence momentum (30%): 10-year annualized GDP-per-capita growth minus that of the high-income group. 50 = keeping pace; each 1 pp/yr of catch-up adds 10 points (capped 0–100).
  • Footprint (25%): mean of carbon intensity score (CO₂ t per $1,000 GDP, 0–0.5) and PM2.5 exposure score (5–65 µg/m³).

Weights are judgemental and meant to be challenged. Known distortions: GDP-based scores overstate economies where multinational profit accounting inflates GDP (e.g. Ireland, Luxembourg), and resource exporters can show volatile momentum. Aggregates (World, regions, income groups) use World Bank values where published; otherwise population-weighted means of members with ≥80% population coverage.

Convergence scenarios Scenario

Current trend extends each economy's 10-year growth differential against the high-income group. Peer pace applies the 80th-percentile differential achieved by low- and middle-income economies over the same window, the "proven peer pace" logic used in Century Pathways (HH-2026-61). "Years to 50%" is when GDP per capita would reach half the high-income level under the current-trend scenario.

Convergence test

Unconditional β-convergence: each dot is an economy; x = GDP per capita (PPP) at the start of the window, y = annualized growth since. A negative slope means poorer economies are growing faster (convergence). Dot area ∝ population.
