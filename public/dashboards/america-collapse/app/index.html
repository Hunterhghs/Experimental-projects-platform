Stage I · Structural Root

Dissolution of the Nuclear Family

Married-with-children households fell from 44.2% (1960) to 17.6% (2024). Below-replacement fertility (US TFR 1.62; NH White 1.53) and median age 44.5 create sustained natural decrease among European-origin cohorts.

Stage II · Demographic Accelerator

Immigration & Ethnic Blending

Net international migration drives >80% of U.S. population growth through 2100. Simultaneously, 19% of new marriages are interracial and the Census Two-or-More-Races population surged from 2.4% (2000) to 10.2% (2020).

Stage III · Spatial Anchor

Majority-Minority Cultural Epicenters

Gateway metros and inner-ring suburbs anchor the shift: New York City (30.9% NH White), Philadelphia (34.4%), Washington D.C. (38.0%), Los Angeles (25.0%), and Houston/Harris (26.0%) radiate outward across 422 counties.

Stage IV · Civilizational Outcome

NH White Decline & PACT Resilience

National NH White share falls from 69.4% (2000) → 57.8% (2020 Census) → <50% (~2050) → ~39% (2100 Main). Long-term stability depends on the PACT Continuity Ratio (Absorption × Civic Core × Trust ÷ Pace).

Empirical Synthesis (Hughes 2026, Zenodo 23596797): Sub-replacement fertility is structural across Western nations. Critically, minority fertility converges rapidly toward native norms across generations (2024 US TFR: Total 1.62, NH White 1.53, Hispanic 1.88, NH Black 1.60, NH Asian 1.42). Thus, the decline in NH White population share is driven not by anomalously high minority birth rates, but by NH White nuclear-family contraction and older age structure (median age 44.5 vs. 31.2 for Hispanics) combined with sustained immigration and intermarriage.

2050 Baseline Verdict: Managed Transformation Under Strain
Western institutions remain intact and recognizable through 2050–2100 while becoming substantially more diverse, but face persistent polarization, fiscal aging pressures, and localized cohesion friction.

The Post-Peak Civilization Thesis (Hughes 2026, HH-2026-49): Western Europe and Western Offshoots rose from 28% of world GDP in 1820 to a historic peak of 57% in 1950, falling to 31% by 2022 as Asia industrialized; Western share of world population fell from 28.8% (1950) to 13.8% (2024) and is projected to reach 10.5% in 2100. The evidence confirms the weak (relative multipolar) thesis while refuting biological/racial degradation claims: human genetic variation lies overwhelmingly within populations, and long-term outcomes are governed by institutional, demographic, and civic adaptation.
